!!! note "This is a [knowledge-management practice](index.md)"
    How knowledge gets organised, independently of any tool that stores it.

Most of what a knowledge base asks of a page is justified. State one idea. Say
why it generalises. Connect it to what it rests on. Earn the claim. Applied to
everything, that standard is also the reason a knowledge base runs dry: at the
moment you have just lost an afternoon to something, the last thing you will do
is work out what it means.

So reserve one tier where **none of it is asked**. A page in that tier does not
have to be general, does not have to connect to anything, and is not required to
earn its place. It has to be true, and it has to have cost someone an afternoon.
That is the whole bar.

## Why the cheap tier is the one that fills

The knowledge worth the most per word is the knowledge nobody would write down
under ceremony: the error message that meant something other than what it said,
the default that changed between versions, the flag whose absence produced a
silent wrong answer. None of it is general. All of it costs an afternoon, twice,
because the second encounter is not recognised as the first.

Ceremony does not improve these pages. It prevents them. A page that would take
four minutes to write and forty to justify does not get written at all, and the
afternoon is paid again later.

## Promotion, not pre-emption

The objection is that a cheap tier fills with material that should have been
thought through. The answer is to let the thinking happen **when it is cheap**,
which is on the second or third encounter rather than the first.

When a page in the cheap tier turns out to carry a reusable claim, the claim is
promoted to wherever reusable claims live, and the cheap page links to it rather
than restating it, per
[Give every cross-cutting concept one definitional home](../principles/give_every_cross_cutting_concept_one_definitional_home.md).
Nothing is lost by starting cheap, because the tier is a staging area with a
promotion path and not a landfill.

Two properties keep it from rotting. The page **names the symptom** in its title,
in the words someone would have searched for, because search is how anyone
arrives. And the tier is listed separately from the rest, since it grows fastest
and reads least as a narrative: a reader following an argument should not have to
scroll past a hundred afternoons.

## The tell that you need one

You know something worth recording, you can feel yourself deciding not to write
it down, and the reason is that you cannot see which category it belongs to or
what it would have to say to be worth a page. That hesitation is the cheap tier
asking to exist. In this bundle it is called
[findings](../findings/index.md).

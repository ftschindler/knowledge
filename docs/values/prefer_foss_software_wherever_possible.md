**Value.** Given a choice between a free and open-source tool and a proprietary
one that both meet the need, prefer the FOSS option.

**Why it matters to me.** Open source is auditable (I can see what it does with my
data), resistant to lock-in and rug-pulls (no vendor can revoke or paywall it out
from under me), and typically longer-lived because anyone can maintain a fork. It
aligns my toolchain with autonomy over my own data and workflow, not a vendor's
roadmap or business model.

**What it implies.** FOSS-first steers concrete tool selection: an open editor and
plugin ecosystem over a closed app, self-hostable or local tooling over
SaaS-only, open formats (which reinforces
[Prefer plain-text, tool-agnostic formats](prefer_plain_text_tool_agnostic_formats.md)). Where a convenience layer is
wanted, prefer one that is itself FOSS and optional.

**Tensions.** Sometimes the proprietary tool is simply better, or the FOSS
alternative doesn't exist or is unmaintained. This is a *preference, not an
absolute* - "wherever possible" is doing real work. When a closed tool wins, prefer
one that at least reads/writes open formats, so the lock-in is in the *tool* and
never in the *data*.
